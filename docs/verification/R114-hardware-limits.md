# R114: model limits on real hardware

Measured on 2026-11-07 on the GPU PC (AMD Radeon RX 6800 XT) with a benchmark-only build at
`SKETCHYUP_BENCHMARK_LIMIT_SCALE=8`. This is a measurement and a recommendation. It changes no
product limit: the same sources at scale 1 report the published limits, and the capability output
is byte-identical to `origin/main` (see [Product limits unchanged](#product-limits-unchanged)).
Machine-readable results are in [R114-hardware-limits.json](R114-hardware-limits.json). Models are
synthetic, generated by the benchmark. This record is not release-candidate evidence
(`releaseAcceptance` is false).

## Summary

- **The frame budget holds further than the edit budget.** A complete frame at 1920 by 1080 meets
  the 33.3 ms p95 budget for up to 2.4 million unique faces, 800,000 faces in one body, 27
  placements of a 176,000-face definition, 512 MiB of textures and 1,024 definitions. It misses for
  repeated placements from 20,000 (34 to 38 ms p95) and above, and at 80,000 placements it is 127 ms.
- **The edit budget (p95 under 100 ms) is the constraint.** Moving one placement or body meets it
  for up to 20,000 placements, 2.4 million unique faces (in 200-face bodies) and 512 MiB of
  textures. It misses for one body from 100,000 faces (351 ms; a linear fit puts 100 ms near
  35,000 faces, which was not measured), for a 176,000-face definition (about 257 ms, whatever
  the placement count), and for 1,024 definitions (268 ms).
- **The undo budget (192 MiB) stops definition edits early.** A definition edit charges the records
  of every placement it regenerates, about 35 KB per placement for a 26-sided prism. It is accepted at
  5,000 placements (176 MB charged) and refused at 10,000 and above. For a 176,000-face definition it
  is refused even with 3 placements. A move of one body charges about 640 bytes per face, so a move
  above about 300,000 faces is refused.
- **Two product caps stop the largest tiers.** The materialized vertex limit (600,000, so 4,800,000
  at scale 8) refuses the 92,308th prism; 160,000 placements never fit. The stored texture total
  (64 MiB, so 512 MiB at scale 8) refuses 1,024 MiB.
- **Two findings the owner should see.** The viewport keeps 128 MiB of decoded textures, which is
  two 4096 by 4096 images. A model at the published 64 MiB stored texture limit with four such
  textures shows placeholders for two of them (measured at scale 1's viewport budget, see
  [What failed](#what-failed-and-how)). Push-pull refuses any body above 1,000 faces, a product
  context limit that is not scaled here, so push-pull on the dense bodies was not measurable.
- **Binary storage (R116) is needed to raise the unique limit above about 900,000 faces,** not to
  meet the frame or edit budgets. The JSON model is about 107 bytes per unique face here (159 bytes
  in R112's short-valued model), so the 96 MiB model chunk holds roughly 630,000 to 940,000
  faces. The 2.4 million-face model is 246 MiB and opens in 6 seconds with 8.0 GB peak RSS.

- `sketchyup-cli --capabilities` from a default (scale 1) build of this branch is byte-identical to
  the same output from a build of `origin/main` 2270b370 (1,658,514 bytes each).
- The existing limit tests pass in the default build: `document_limits_tests`,
  `document_limits_io_tests`, `component_placement_tests`, `unique_limits_acceptance_tests` (at-limit
  model saves, reopens and undoes byte for byte), and `snapshot_glb_limits_acceptance_tests` (at-limit

- Every scaled value is `N * DocumentLimits::scale`, and the scale is defined only by the benchmark's
  CMake flags. Product targets never define it.

### Binary storage (R116)

The unique family measures the model JSON at 107 bytes per unique face. The 2.4 million-face model is
258 MB of JSON (246 MiB in the container). That is the answer to the brief's question:

- The viewport and edit budgets do not need a binary format. At 2.4 million faces the frame p95 is
  18.5 ms, the move p95 is 34.6 ms and the undo p95 is 31 ms.
- The file does need one above the 96 MiB model chunk. At 107 bytes per face that is about 940,000
  unique faces, and at the 159 bytes per face of R112's short-valued model, about 630,000 faces.
  Raising the unique limit above that needs a new document schema (R116).
- The 2.4 million-face open takes 6.1 s and peaks at 8.0 GB RSS. A binary format would cut the parse
  cost, but nothing measured here shows that the parse is the limiting cost. This is not measured,
  because no binary format exists.

Verdict: R116 is justified for any raise of the unique limit above about 900,000 faces, and not
justified by the frame or edit budgets measured here.

## What failed and how

Each failure below is in the results, not hidden. The first attempts of two families were
discarded and the harness corrected; the discarded runs are not counted in any table.

1. **Repeated placements, 160,000.** Refused by the fixture at the 92,308th placement: "vertices
   would be 4,800,016 of 4,800,000". The family stopped at 80,000.
2. **Definition edits, every placement count from 10,000 up.** Refused by the undo budget: "Edit
   exceeds the 192 MiB history budget". The charge is the definition's records and the records of its
   placements, as ADR 0005 describes, so a definition edit at 5,000 placements charges 175.7 MB.
3. **Definition edits of a 176,000-face definition.** Refused at 3 placements (the same message).
4. **Moves of one body above about 300,000 faces.** Refused at 400,000 and 800,000 faces (the same
   message). At 800,000 faces the record has frame, pick and inference figures, and no edit figure.
5. **Push-pull on any body above 1,000 faces.** Refused: "Push/pull exceeds the 1000-face/10000-vertex
   context limit". This is a product limit the benchmark does not scale. Push-pull on the dense
   bodies was not measured. Push-pull on the textured prisms and on the unique family's 200-face
   bodies was measured.
6. **Push-pull at the unique limit.** The 2,400,000-face model is at the limit, so a push-pull that
   adds 3 faces is refused: "faces would be 2,400,003 of 2,400,000". This is the limit working, not a
   speed result.
7. **One dense body, 800,000 faces, first attempt.** The first attempt failed on its first frame:
   "Viewport unavailable: Shading exceeds two million face corners". A body of 800,000 faces has
   2.4 million corners, and the shading-normal cap of 2,000,000 corners per body is a literal the brief
   did not list. The cap is now scaled in benchmark builds (`2000000 * DocumentLimits::scale`), and
   the tier was run again: three measured runs, the results above. The earlier attempt is not counted.
8. **Textures, first campaign.** The viewport reported "Some images are unavailable; reference planes
   show a purple placeholder" at every tier of 64 MiB and above. The cause is the viewport's decoded
   texture budget, 128 MiB, which holds two 4096 by 4096 RGBA images (64 MiB each). Those runs
   measured the placeholder, not textures, so they are discarded. The budget is now scaled in
   benchmark builds (128 MiB times scale squared, 8 GiB at scale 8), and the harness fails any case
   whose textures are unavailable, so a placeholder run cannot be recorded. The 64 to 512 MiB tiers
   were run again and are the results above.
9. **Textures, 512 MiB GLB export.** Refused: "GLB decoded texture images exceed 256 MiB". The
   message is a literal from the product; at scale 8 the actual cap is 2 GiB (binaryLimit scaled), and
   the decoded images are 2.37 GB. The message text is stale for benchmark builds and correct for the
   product.
10. **Textures, 1,024 MiB.** Refused by the fixture at the stored total: "Assets exceed the 64 MiB
    document budget". The message is a product literal; at scale 8 the total is 512 MiB. The refusal
    happened at the 37th texture, with 507 MiB stored.
11. **Definitions, 4,096.** The fixture build did not finish in 30 minutes. The case was stopped by the
    time limit (`Finished with result: timeout`, status 15). The 1,024-definition build took 533 s.
    The family stopped there, so 16,384 was not run.
12. **Placements, first campaign.** Four harness faults were found and fixed before the results above
    were taken. (a) The fixture's own build history was charged to the first edit (201 MB at 10,000
    placements, so a move sat at the undo budget); the working document is now the reopened file, with an
    empty history. (b) Edits were not undone before the next, so one edit's charge accumulated; each edit is now
    undone and redone before the next. (c) The unique face count included projected placement copies; it
    now counts unique geometry as the product does. (d) The render snapshot and GLB vertex-reference caps
    (2,000,000 at product) refused the 20,000-placement export; they are now scaled in benchmark builds.
    The runs made before these fixes are not counted.

Two refusals are not limits the benchmark can scale: the push-pull context limit (item 5) and the
quadratic face-pair budget in push-pull (`checkCoplanarOverlap`, 1,000,000 pairs), which refuses a body
above about 1,414 faces at any scale. Scaling the context limit alone would move the stop to that
budget, so neither was scaled.

## Not run, and why

- **Definitions, 16,384.** Not run: the 4,096 tier stopped the family at its 30-minute limit.
- **Repeated placements, 160,000.** Not built: the materialized vertex limit refuses it (item 1).
- **Textures, 1,024 MiB.** Not built: the stored total refuses it (item 10).
- **Definition edits, every tier.** Refused by the undo budget (item 2 and 3). Their feedback
  figures are therefore absent from the tables.
- **Push-pull on any body above 1,000 faces.** Refused by the product context limit (item 5).
- **Push-pull at 2,400,000 faces.** Refused by the unique limit (item 6).
- **GLB export of textures at 512 MiB.** Refused by the decoded-texture cap (item 9).
- **The sharing-off control and the Intel machine.** Outside this brief. GPU memory is whole-GPU,
  not per process.

## Risks

- The textures are generated 2-bit noise with a distinct `tEXt` chunk per texture. They encode to
  about 14.6 MB each, a little under the 16 MiB published limit, and decode as the viewport decodes any
  4096 by 4096 PNG. Real photographs are larger or smaller and may behave differently.
- The fixture of each family is built in process by the document's own API; the timings of the
  fixture are the product's creation cost, which is slow for many definitions (item 11).
- The campaign measured one machine: an RX 6800 XT under Mesa 27.0.0-devel on a guest. The owner's
  laptop (Intel) and other GPUs were not measured.
- The record's VRAM and GTT columns are whole-GPU samples. Another process used the GPU during part
  of the campaign; the waits are in the JSON.

## Contradictions with the design records

- **ADR 0005, "History budget" (2026-11-01).** The ADR says that every definition's edit fits the
  budget once a definition is limited to 200,000 faces. The measurements show that a definition edit
  also charges the records of its placements: 176 MB at 5,000 placements of a 26-sided prism, and a
  176,000-face definition is refused at 3 placements. The owner's decision was framed on definition
  faces alone. [Implementation notes (R114)](../decisions/0005-document-protocol.md#implementation-notes-r114-measured-limits)
  records this.
- **The 20,000-placement limit.** The 600,000 materialized vertex limit stops 26-sided prisms at 11,538
  placements. The 20,000 placement limit is not reachable with that shape, and the record states it.
- **The viewport texture budget against the stored texture limit.** A model within the 64 MiB stored
  limit can show placeholders, because the viewport holds 128 MiB decoded. This is a product issue, not
  a limit set by the design records, and no design record sets the decoded budget.
